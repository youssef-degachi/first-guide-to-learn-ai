## The idea

Choose a small project that matters to your audience: a source-grounded lesson, a checked data report, a useful app flow, or a controlled media edit. Your final deliverable should let another person reproduce the important step and judge its result.

Use the six-week plan in the [teaching playbook](#/resources/teaching-playbook). It is a suggested schedule; take longer when a mastery check fails.

Before teaching a topic, you should be able to:

1. Explain it in plain language without a vendor slogan.
2. Complete a relevant task yourself.
3. Demonstrate a failure or limitation and a check that detects it.
4. State the model, app, access level, and date used in your demo.
5. Give learners a small exercise and evaluate their result fairly.
6. Distinguish your observation from a vendor claim and an untested idea.

Teaching readiness comes from a reliable process: clear foundations, tested examples, and the ability to investigate unfamiliar cases. State the scope and limits of each experiment.

For weekly updates, maintain a simple registry: product, exact version, change date, availability, official source, your test, result, and next review date. Prioritize changes that affect a lesson or workflow. A renamed button is less important than a changed access restriction, retired endpoint, new editing capability, or altered cost.

Your next action is practical: complete the first three labs, save the evidence, and explain one result to a learner. Build the next lesson from what the learner could reproduce.

### Your project pack

Include the task brief, inputs, sources, actual outputs, checks, unresolved limitations, a short teaching script, and a learner exercise with an answer or rubric. Keep product versions and research dates visible.

### A final teach-back

Ask a learner to complete a comparable task. Their result is feedback on both the tool and your explanation. Revise the lesson before treating it as a reusable tutorial.

## Try it

**Time/access:** 60–90 minutes preparation plus a 20-minute session; any completed lab.

Choose a topic you have tested. Prepare a short explanation, source note, demonstration, learner exercise, expected answer, and limitation. Teach one learner without taking over their attempt.

**Check:** Can they reproduce the key result and explain why it works? Use an unseen but comparable example to test transfer. Ask what remained confusing and revise the lesson.

**Save/teach:** A lesson pack and an honest report of learner outcomes. This is your readiness check for publishing an educational episode on the topic.

## Check your work

The learner should complete an unseen comparable task and explain the success check without your intervention.

Keep the [instructor answer key](#/resources/instructor-key) separate until you finish the attempt. Open the [full lab workbook](#/resources/practice-labs) for related exercises.

## Teach it

Share the evidence and what you changed after teaching, then choose your next experiment.
