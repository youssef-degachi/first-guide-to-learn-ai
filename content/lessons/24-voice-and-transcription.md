## The idea

Generated speech, transcription, and live voice have different jobs. Use a known passage so you can measure accuracy as well as naturalness. Dates, prices, names, and corrections deserve their own inspection.

Distinguish transcription, generated speech, and live conversation. Transcription turns recorded speech into text; speech generation reads text; a live voice system manages an ongoing exchange and may delegate work to tools.

Current examples include **Eleven v4/v4 Turbo** for speech and OpenAI's **GPT-Live** conversation system. OpenAI's live guide describes a voice model working with a backend agent for reasoning and tools. [ElevenLabs announcement](https://elevenlabs.io/docs/changelog/2026/9/28) · [Live guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/live)

Test dates, names, prices, pauses, and corrections. Test the languages and code-switching patterns the intended audience uses, and record the language and recording conditions. Use your own voice or an appropriately licensed voice. A convincing voice is not evidence that a spoken claim is correct.

## Try it

**Time/access:** 20–40 minutes; Media/TTS.

Use the fixed voice passage in the media briefs with your own or a licensed voice. Generate once, identify specific delivery issues, then revise only the directions or pronunciation controls available in the tool.

**Check:** Date, price, wording, pacing, and intelligibility. Have a listener unfamiliar with the passage write down the date and price they heard.

**Save/teach:** The passage, first output, revised output, and listener feedback. Expressiveness and factual preservation are separate checks.

## Check your work

Ask a listener to write the date and price they heard. Compare a transcript with the fixed passage and mark errors.
