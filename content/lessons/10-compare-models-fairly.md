## The idea

A useful comparison starts with representative tasks and predeclared checks. Record tool access and the time spent fixing the result. If one app can browse and the other cannot, you are comparing workflows as well as models.

Write down the task before opening the model menu. Then compare:

1. **Correctness:** Does it satisfy the requirements?
2. **Grounding:** Can important claims be traced to evidence?
3. **Instruction following:** Does it preserve the format, scope, and constraints?
4. **Time:** How long until a usable result, including your corrections?
5. **Cost:** What did the complete attempt cost, including retries and tools?
6. **Fit:** Can the app actually read your files or perform the required operation?

A model that produces a fine draft quickly can be excellent for rewriting but unsuitable for unattended decisions. A more expensive model may reduce rework on difficult tasks. Both are hypotheses to measure. OpenAI's selection guide likewise recommends evaluating against the actual workload. [Model selection](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/model-selection)

## Try it

**Time/access:** 45–75 minutes; two accessible model choices.

Choose Labs 4, 5, and 6 as a small task set. Use identical materials and evaluation rules. Keep tool access aligned if comparing models; otherwise label it an app comparison. Run fresh attempts and log corrections.

**Check:** Report per-task results and the size of the sample. Include time to a usable result. Do not turn one successful arithmetic answer into a claim that a model is best at everything.

**Save/teach:** A small comparison card: setup, tasks, success rate, corrections, time, and limitations. Carbon, Argon, Hydrogen, Sol, Claude, or Grok are candidates only if you can actually access them.

## Check your work
