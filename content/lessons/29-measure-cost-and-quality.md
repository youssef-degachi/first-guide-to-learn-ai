## The idea

The complete cost of a result includes retries, tools, waiting, and human correction. A fast first response may still lead to slow work. Record the time to a usable result and essential task failures separately from a quality score.

### Measure the workflow

Log input, model/app, tools, output, attempts, elapsed time, corrections, and credits or cost when visible. Use unknown for unavailable values rather than estimating silently.

### Keep the conclusion narrow

A few examples can reveal a failure mode but do not establish universal superiority. Compare per-task outcomes and explain important differences in setup.

### Improve one bottleneck

Change one variable: context, task brief, model, tool access, or checking method. Re-run the relevant cases. Adopt the change when the evidence supports it. Use the [experiment log template](#/resources/experiment-log) and retain failed attempts.

## Try it

**Time/access:** 45–75 minutes; two accessible model choices.

Choose Labs 4, 5, and 6 as a small task set. Use identical materials and evaluation rules. Keep tool access aligned if comparing models; otherwise label it an app comparison. Run fresh attempts and log corrections.

**Check:** Report per-task results and the size of the sample. Include time to a usable result. Do not turn one successful arithmetic answer into a claim that a model is best at everything.

**Save/teach:** A small comparison card: setup, tasks, success rate, corrections, time, and limitations. Carbon, Argon, Hydrogen, Sol, Claude, or Grok are candidates only if you can actually access them.
