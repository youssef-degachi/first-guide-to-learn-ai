# AI from zero to teacher: start here

**AI Fieldnotes · Research snapshot: 14 November 2026**

This learning kit introduces practical AI use from foundational concepts to advanced workflows, teaching, and content production. It connects current names to practical skills, experiments, and lessons.

The goal is to be able to explain a method, demonstrate it, detect when it fails, and help another person repeat it. Memorizing every new model name will not achieve that goal.

## What “current” means here

Model and product facts were checked against official documentation, repositories, and announcements on **14 November 2026**. Dated announcements are distinguished from wider availability, previews, and future pricing changes. The model-family section was reviewed again on **15 November 2026**; other sections retain the original research snapshot. A release can change after either review date. Follow the source beside the claim before recording a news episode.

The model tables are a selected map of important families, not an exhaustive list or an independent ranking. Vendor claims establish what a vendor reports. The curriculum and labs provide a suggested learning and teaching progression.

**These are research-backed instructions, not completed comparisons of every AI product.** No paid generation, live cross-model benchmark, account connection, or installation was performed to prepare the kit. The arithmetic and contact-cleaning answer checks in the supplied fixtures were verified with deterministic calculations. The exercise data is fictional.

## A sensible starting setup

Start with one general AI app, a folder for your experiments, and a simple score sheet. Add a source-grounded research tool when you reach the research labs. Add an image, voice, or video tool only for the relevant media experiment. Add a coding agent or workflow builder when you have a task and a check for its output.

Choose your first paid subscription only after identifying a repeated limitation in your practice. Record whether the limitation is model quality, quota, missing tools, file handling, or workflow friction; each calls for a different solution.

Your most useful weekly habit is one verified experiment and one clearly explained lesson. The name of the newest model matters less than knowing what changed, whether you can access it, and whether it improves your work.
